      How this works

      Routes are computed in your browser over /*__N_ARCS__*/ street segments, with USGS 1 m lidar elevation on the Overture / OpenStreetMap street graph. The slider runs the whole trade-off between distance and climbing: every route nothing else beats on both, from the shortest to the flattest worth walking. Sliding right never shortens the route and never adds climbing. Climbing is total gain, not the difference between the endpoints. Stairs count on foot, not on a bike.

      The loop button turns the trip into a loop from one start: the slider sets the distance, dozens of loops of about that length are tried in every direction and the flattest is shown; tick allow out and back to include the same streets there and back, which is often flatter still. On a bike, prefer calm streets counts protected lanes and paths (Atlanta Regional Commission inventory) as shorter than they are and busy arterials as longer.

      Avoid Flock cameras keeps the route out of view of every automated license plate reader on DeFlock, the crowd-sourced map (most are Flock Safety's; all makes are avoided). A camera is taken to see 40 m down the direction it faces and 10 m all round, and a block is closed if any of it is in view. Where no route avoids them all, the one in view for the least distance is used and the page says how many it passes. The page carries a snapshot, and ticking the box asks OpenStreetMap's Overpass API for the current map, which is the only request this page ever makes to anyone else. A camera nobody has mapped is not avoided.

      Avoid high-crime areas keeps the route off the blocks with the most reported violent crime: robberies, assaults and the like in public places over the last two years, from the Atlanta Police Department's incident reports. Crimes at home and between family are left out, as is all property crime. About one street in twenty-five is closed. It works the way the cameras do: those blocks are avoided first, and where a trip starts or ends among them the way through that spends the least distance on them is used and marked. These are counts of reports, not the odds of anything happening to you, and a busy street has more of them partly for being busy.

      The target button on the map shows where you are, with an arrow for the way you are facing, and keeps the map on you as you walk; drag the map to look elsewhere and tap it again to come back. Your position stays on your phone: it is never sent anywhere.

      Place search is offline: the city's intersections, places and addresses are built into this page. Routes stay inside the Atlanta city limits. Source, data and the full analysis.
